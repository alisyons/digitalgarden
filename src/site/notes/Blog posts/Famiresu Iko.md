> This post includes spoilers for all of Famiresu Iko! If you want to read this manga (which I highly recommend), I advise you to do so and come back later. It's a quick read!

I watched Karaoke Iko yesterday with my friend. It was a fun, somewhat lighthearted comedy / coming of age story about a middle schooler having to teach a yakuza how to sing because of his crazy boss. Never would I have expected a sequel that changes so much in tone and explores the relationship between these characters after a time skip. 

_Famiresu Iko_ (Let's Go To A Family Restaurant) takes place in... well, mostly a family restaurant where Satomi is working part time now that he is in college. And yet he finds himself meeting up with Kyouji on the regular for dinner. The nature of these meetups is never stated to be of any particular intent, they just hang out, eat and banter in their usual ways. 
![famiresu4.jpg](/img/user/images/famiresu4.jpg)
I really enjoyed the plot unravelling around them, related to the yakuza boss's lost son, an investigative journalist and the manga publishing industry. All in all, almost every side character becomes relevant in the great scheme of things, and even the less prominent members of the cast get characterized in a way that makes you remember them. This work thrives off the interactions and their authenticity. It's masterfully done slice of life that doesn't get boring.

![famiresu3.png|500](/img/user/images/famiresu3.png)

However, this manga truly shines in how the relationship between Kyouji and Satomi plays out. Now, with the framing of it all, I feel like a lot of people would wonder: _Is this a BL?_ And to that I have to answer: _I really don't know._ The more I think about it, the more I realize that this should not be the main takeway of the story, nor should you expect it to be or not to be a BL, depending on how comfortable you are with the dynamic between the two. There's people who see them in a familial uncle/nephew dynamic, there's people who see them in a romantic light, but ultimately, everyone agrees that they share some kind of bond that is hard to describe and transcends traditional notions of love. 

The manga itself gives neither side a bulletproof vest. It constantly tiptoes around any concrete naming of what these two have going on. What started with Satomi giving Kyouji a hug and then frantically googling what it means to hug someone and how to make somebody quit the yakuza, culminates in a simple "I suppose he likes him." We never get to know Kyouji's side of things as a central question never gets answered:

![famiresu5.jpg|500](/img/user/images/famiresu5.jpg)
We don't know what Kyouji would get out of this relationship. His point of view is forever obscured behind his go-with-the-flow mindset and nonchalant attitude. To me, this is a story about a queer young man coming to terms with his crush on an older man. Wayama Yama, the mangaka, said that she wrote this sequel with Satomi's happiness in mind. First, this is hard to understand since the ending is very bittersweet. Satomi gets a lukewarm hug from Kyouji and the two part ways. But the alternative is that Satomi would be running after a man who is unavailable both emotionally and socially due to the taboo nature of their relationship. In that sense, he prioritizes his happiness and takes the necessary step back.

Wayama Yama announced that her next work will be published in 2027. We don't know if this will be another sequel or a standalone work alltogether. Either way, I'm excited to read whatever she will write and in the meantime I will work through her backlog of manga.
